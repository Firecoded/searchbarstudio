The shape of it

Two products, one domain

Public

Marketing site

The "hire me" front end. Hero, services, care-plan pitch, work, about, contact. Its whole job is to get a qualified message into your inbox. This is the mockup we designed.

Private

Client portal & back office

Your admin plus client logins, invites, and billing status. The thing that gets you paid. Built on the same auth and database, shipped in a later phase.

The stack

What runs it, and why

Framework
Next.js on Vercel
One framework for the marketing site and the app, clean path for both the front end and the Stripe/auth server bits.
Vercel's free Hobby tier is technically non-commercial. Once you're charging clients, move to Pro (~$20/mo) or a cheap VPS. Not a day-one problem.
Auth
Better Auth
Email + password with a reset flow (you chose this over magic links, password managers make it frictionless). You own the data, no per-user pricing. Magic links stay available as a plugin later.
Its admin and organization plugins cover "manage users." Invites = a one-time "set your password" link, same mechanism as a reset token.
Database
Neon serverless Postgres
Free tier is plenty (0.5 GB holds thousands of clients). Chosen over Supabase because Supabase's free tier fully pauses after ~7 days idle, Neon just briefly cold-starts.
Compute sleeps after 5 min idle and wakes in ~0.5s. Hide it with a warm-up ping when the login page loads. Kill it entirely later with Neon's always-on (~$19/mo) if it ever bugs you.
Email
Resend
Transactional email for invites, password resets, and contact-form submissions. Free tier (3k/mo) is far more than you'll send. Best Next.js DX (React email templates).
One setup task: verify your domain (DNS records) so mail lands in inboxes, not spam.
Payments
Stripe Billing / Subscriptions
Hosted checkout + hosted billing portal do the entire recurring-money job: store the card, charge monthly forever, retry failures, email receipts, let clients update their card.
No card data ever touches your code. Your app only reads subscription status from Stripe and displays it ("Active, next charge Sept 28").
Accounts

Who logs in

  • Admin (you). The only admin. Manage clients, send invites, kick off subscriptions, read billing status.
  • Clients. Regular users. Get an invite email → set a password → land on their own dashboard (project, invoices, subscription status).
  • Invite flow. You enter a client's email; they get a one-time link to set their password. No temp passwords, no manual account creation.
The money path

How a $/month client gets set up

Build ships

Site is done, time to start the recurring plan.

Create the subscription

In your admin (or the Stripe dashboard early on), create the customer + monthly subscription and send the link.

Client subscribes

They open Stripe's hosted checkout, enter their card once, and confirm. Stripe stores it and charges immediately.

It runs itself

Stripe charges every month forever, emails receipts, and handles failed-card retries and card updates through its billing portal.

Your app just reflects it

The dashboard reads status from Stripe and shows "Active, next charge on [date]." You never build billing logic.

Build order

Ship in two phases

Version A

Build first
  • Marketing site (the mockup, turned real) + your admin + Stripe subscriptions.
  • You build your own auth login from day one anyway, so "login" exists regardless.
  • Lets you charge real clients in days, managing them from the Stripe dashboard at first.

Version B

Grow into it
  • Client-facing dashboards + polished invite flow, added when a client would actually use one.
  • Because the auth was built in Version A, adding clients is a small step, not a rebuild.
  • Becomes the home for upsells: "request a change," add-ons, self-serve.

Delivery, not your own stack

How you build client sites

Keep this separate from everything above: your own site is Next.js. The sites you build for clients are a per-project delivery choice, often WordPress so they can self-edit, sometimes custom.
  • Hybrid editing posture. Deliver sites clients can edit themselves (a dashboard / WordPress), and offer done-for-you edits through the care plan's support hours.
  • Why it works for you. Self-edit makes the "change this text" email optional, and when they do send it, it's billed against support hours. You're never the free-edits guy.
  • Care-plan value leans on hosting, security, and maintenance (things clients can't DIY), plus optional edit labor.
Off the site, for reference

Positioning & pricing (internal)

  • No prices on the site. No figures, no "starting at" ranges. Pure book/contact CTA, the quality of the site is the qualifier. Pricing is handled in outreach.
  • Build fee scales with complexity (rough ballpark $500–$2,000), 50% deposit up front, balance on launch. Recurring starts at launch.
  • Recurring tiers by support hours + maintenance level (not traffic). Base ~1 hr/mo of edits; over that is hourly. New features = scoped mini-quotes, not free edits.
  • Founding-client discounts early to build the portfolio; ask for testimonials + referrals in exchange. Word-of-mouth is the main channel.
  • Positioning: websites are the homepage; web/mobile/custom apps are framed as depth ("Beyond websites"), not a competing menu, because you're a senior full-stack dev.
